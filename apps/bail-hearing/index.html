Bail Hearing Run-Sheet

A step-by-step structure for running a bail hearing under the Criminal Code, anchored on R. v. Antic, 2017 SCC 27. It is built to teach a lawyer new to bail work the sequence of the hearing, and to give an experienced counsel a reliable reminder and checklist on the day. Work top to bottom: each step is something you actually do at the hearing. POC — verify all citations and local program details on the day. Not for client use.
New to bail? Read the green "what you're doing here" notes in each step. Done this before? Skim the headings and the ladder; print it for court.

1 Step 1 — Set the matter

Matter & charge context

Capture what the accused is charged with and where you are.

Use the offence entered above to pick the charge context. Some listed offences reverse the onus by themselves; others reverse it only if an extra fact is present. If no listed trigger fits, leave the default Crown-onus selection.

2 Step 2 — Fix the onus

Who bears the onus

The offence and charge context entered in Step 1 decide the formal burden. Start with the offence charged, then ask whether a listed reverse-onus category or extra circumstance flips the default Crown onus.

Common reverse-onus check — s. 515(6)(a)(i). If the accused is charged with an indictable offence allegedly committed while already at large on another indictable charge, the onus reverses. “At large” just means released/not in custody; no breach is required.
Operational test (s. 515 default rule + s. 515(6) reverse-onus categories)
    3 Step 3 — Meet the three grounds

    The three grounds that justify pre-trial detention (s. 515(10))

    Detention can only rest on one of these three grounds. Address each one separately and do not let the Crown blur them together. If detention is sought on the tertiary ground, answer the St-Cloud factors expressly.

    What you're doing here

    Treat the three grounds as three separate arguments. Concede nothing globally — a release plan that answers the primary ground may say nothing about the secondary or tertiary.

    What to say: "I will address each ground in turn." Then take them one at a time and show how your proposed plan (Step 5) answers each identified risk.

    4 Step 4 — Climb the ladder, least-onerous first

    The Antic ladder of liberty constriction

    Before you climb — is a ground engaged?

    You only reach this ladder if at least one of the three grounds in Step 3 is engaged. If the Crown — or, on a reverse onus, the accused — establishes no primary, secondary, or tertiary concern, the accused is released, by default unconditionally under s. 515(1), and you do not climb at all. If a ground is engaged, the ladder is how you test whether release can address it: start at the least onerous form, move up only as far as necessary, and tie every condition to the specific ground in play (R. v. Zora, 2020 SCC 14). Detention is the top rung — ordered only if no form of release can adequately address the ground. It is not a conclusion you reach before climbing; it is where you end up only when nothing on the ladder works.

    Each rung must be inadequate before the next is imposed (Antic ¶67). Open at rung 1; force the Crown to show cause for each step up (unless reverse onus — then the onus is yours).

    What you're doing here

    Start at the bottom rung — the least onerous form of release — and make the Crown justify every step up (unless reverse onus — then the onus is yours). This is the heart of running a bail hearing well: you never volunteer a higher rung.

    What to say: "The starting point is release on an undertaking. The Crown has not shown why a more onerous form is necessary (unless reverse onus — then the onus is yours)." Then resist each step the Crown seeks, rung by rung.

        5 Step 5 — Put forward the plan of release

        Plan of release — conditions, sureties & bail programs

        Bring a concrete, written plan: proposed address, sureties (only if necessary, not merely useful), employment and community ties, and a local bail program as the lesser-restrictive alternative to a personal surety.

        What you're doing here

        The plan should be as detailed as possible, but delivered viva voce. At rung 5 of the ladder, canvass the local programs below before conceding a surety.

        What to say: walk the court through the plan condition by condition, citing Zora on each: reasonably necessary, no more onerous than required, with a factual nexus to a proven risk.

        Local bail programs (alternative to a personal surety)

        At rung 5, canvass these before agreeing to a personal surety. Confirm eligibility and intake hours on the day.

        Before stepping up

        • Read the synopsis. Confirm what the Crown is alleging.
        • Confirm onus (use Step 2 above).
        • Note any reverse-onus that triggers s. 515(6); plan to address all three grounds at s. 515(10).
        • Identify Indigenous status (Gladue / Ipeelee) and any cultural / community supports.
        • Identify mental-health / fitness flags; ask about medication and substance issues.
        • Get sureties' names + ages + occupations + relationship + financial position; line up backup sureties.
        • Verify residence, employment, school, treatment commitments.
        • Check eligibility for the local bail program (above).

        At the hearing

        • State accused's name and the charges.
        • Confirm onus on the record.
        • Open at rung 1 of the Antic ladder. Force Crown to show cause for each step up (unless reverse onus — then the onus is yours).
        • Address each detention ground separately; do not let Crown blur primary / secondary / tertiary.
        • If detention sought on tertiary: respond to St-Cloud factors expressly.
        • Cite Zora on every condition: must be reasonably necessary, no more onerous than required, with factual nexus.
        • Avoid cash deposits unless accused is non-resident or 200+ km from court (s. 515(2)(d)).
        • If surety required, confirm it is necessary (not merely useful) per Antic ¶67(g).

        After release / detention order

        • If released: review every condition with the accused; explain breach exposure under s. 145.
        • If detained: confirm s. 525 review timing — 90 days for indictable, 30 days for summary.
        • Note any s. 520 / s. 521 review options and ask client about timing.
        • Update file with the recognizance and conditions; calendar the next appearance.
